Hairdressing Courses Near Me UK

23 November 2026

Hairdressing courses near me UK are the essential starting point for anyone wanting to enter or progress in the hair and beauty industry. Whether you’re a school leaver, career changer, or passionate about styling, finding the right local or national training can launch your journey towards becoming a qualified hairdresser in the United Kingdom.

Quick answer: Searching for hairdressing courses near me in the UK will bring up accredited training at local colleges, private academies and through apprenticeships. These cover the practical and theory skills you need to become a professional hairdresser, lead to recognised UK qualifications, and combine classroom learning with hands-on salon experience.

Key Takeaways

  • Multiple pathways exist across the United Kingdom: further education colleges, private academies, and apprenticeships.

  • Accredited hairdressing courses provide nationally recognised qualifications, essential for salon employment.

  • Course fees vary widely by provider and level. Apprenticeship training is funded by the employer and government, and many 16 to 18-year-olds study free at college.

  • Practical experience is integral to all high-quality hairdressing training programmes.

  • Researching course reviews, curriculum standards, and progression routes is key to making an informed choice.

Types of Hairdressing Courses Near Me UK

Further Education Colleges

Further education (FE) colleges across the UK offer hairdressing courses that typically lead to a Level 2 or Level 3 NVQ or Diploma. They balance classroom theory, such as hair science and health and safety, with plenty of hands-on practice in student salons. Full-time courses usually last one to two years, with some part-time options for adults or people already working.

Private Hairdressing Academies

Private academies often run intensive, fast-track courses in and around major UK cities. These can suit career changers or anyone who wants to build skills quickly. Accredited private schools may offer shorter, more flexible schedules and smaller classes. Fees are generally higher than at a college, so check exactly which qualification you will come away with.

Hairdressing Apprenticeships

A hairdressing apprenticeship combines paid work in a salon with study towards a recognised qualification. It is a good way to gain real experience, learn from professional stylists and earn a wage while you train. A Level 2 hairdressing apprenticeship typically takes around two years and is open to school leavers and adults. The training is funded by the employer and government, so it costs the apprentice nothing.

Salon-Based and In-House Training Courses

Some salons, especially larger chains and high-end independents, run their own in-house training. This may focus on advanced techniques, creative styling or specialist skills such as hair extensions. It does not always lead to a formal qualification, but it is valuable for staff development and for experienced stylists who want to widen their skills. Hairdressing Qualifications and Curriculum Standards in the UK

Recognised Hairdressing Qualifications UK

The most widely recognised qualifications are NVQs (National Vocational Qualifications) and Diplomas at Level 2 and Level 3, awarded by bodies such as City & Guilds and VTCT. Most UK salons look for these. Level 2 covers core skills, including cutting, colouring, styling and client care, while Level 3 adds creative work, advanced techniques and the basics of running a salon.

What to Expect in the Hairdressing Curriculum

Course content reflects current industry practice. Modules typically include shampooing and conditioning, blow-drying, basic and advanced cutting, colouring, perming and client consultation. Health and safety, hygiene and customer service run throughout. You are assessed through practical demonstrations, written work and observations on real clients.

Accreditation and Course Quality

Make sure your chosen course is accredited, so your qualification is recognised nationally and meets industry standards. Accredited courses are checked by the awarding body. For an independent view of quality, read the Ofsted report for a college or funded training provider, and look for independent student reviews of private academies.

Progression Routes and Continuous Development

After Level 2, many students progress to Level 3 or to specialist courses in barbering, colour correction or bridal styling. Some move into teaching or salon management, or start their own business. Short courses, masterclasses and trade events help you keep learning, and our post on the top beauty trends in the UK shows what clients are asking for.

Costs, Funding, and Comparing Hairdressing Training Options

How Much Do Hairdressing Courses Cost in the UK?

Fees vary a great deal with the type of course, the provider and the location, so always check the provider’s own fee page. College courses are often free for 16 to 18-year-olds, while adults may pay a fee or use a loan. Private academies set their own prices for intensive or specialist programmes. Fees usually cover tuition, use of the training salon and assessment, but may not include equipment or uniform. Apprenticeship training costs the apprentice nothing.

What’s Included in Course Fees?

Check exactly what the advertised fee includes. Some courses provide a starter kit (scissors, brushes and styling tools), textbooks and uniform. Others charge extra for equipment or exam resits. Always ask for a full breakdown.

Funding, Bursaries, and Government Support

In England, 16 to 18-year-olds can usually study free at a state-funded college. Adults aged 19 or over may be able to get an Advanced Learner Loan for a Level 3 course, or other support depending on their circumstances. Apprenticeship training is funded by the employer and government. Check with your local provider and on gov.uk for the latest funding rules, which differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

How to Compare Hairdressing Course Reviews

When comparing hairdressing schools, reviews are useful. Look for consistent praise or criticism of teaching quality, facilities and support. What past students went on to do, such as salon jobs or their own business, is a strong sign of a provider’s reputation.

Choosing the Right Hairdressing Course for Your Goals

Assessing Your Needs and Career Ambitions

Before choosing a course, consider your long-term aims: do you want to work in a high street salon, specialise in bridal styling, or run your own business? Each path may require different qualifications or advanced training. Some courses focus on foundational skills, while others offer modules in creative colour, extensions, or editorial styling. For inspiration, check out our advice on choosing a hair colour to suit your skin tone.

Location, Flexibility, and Learning Style

You can find hairdressing courses in most towns and cities, but location, travel costs and timetable all matter. Full-time, part-time, evening and weekend options are available to suit different lifestyles. Private academies may offer one-to-one tuition, while colleges offer a more social, structured setting. Think about which suits the way you learn.

Industry Links and Work Experience

Strong industry links are a good sign. Look for providers that offer work experience, guest sessions from working stylists or links with local salons, as this network can lead to a job. Many courses include work-based elements or supervised client appointments.

Support Services and Progression Opportunities

Ask about student support, such as mentoring, careers advice and help with job applications. Clear routes from Level 2 to Level 3 and on to advanced workshops matter for a lasting career. Applying for Hairdressing Courses: Step-by-Step Guide

Entry Requirements for UK Hairdressing Courses

Entry requirements vary by provider. Some Level 2 courses ask for GCSEs, often including English and maths, while others look mainly for enthusiasm and commitment at interview. For an apprenticeship, you need a salon willing to take you on. Mature students are usually welcome.

Application Process

Applications are typically made online via the provider’s website. You may need to write a personal statement detailing your interest in hairdressing, provide references, and sometimes attend an interview or assessment. For apprenticeships, the process includes finding an employer willing to support your training. Check deadlines, as popular courses fill fast—especially in major UK cities.

Preparing for Your Hairdressing Course

Once accepted, you’ll receive a list of required equipment—such as scissors, combs, styling brushes, and mannequin heads. Some colleges and academies supply student kits, while others expect you to purchase your own. Reading up on hair care, such as our tips for dry scalp, can give you an advantage before starting.

What to Expect on Day One and Beyond

Your first weeks will cover health and safety, basic hair science, and introductory practical tasks like shampooing and blow-drying. As you progress, you’ll move on to cutting, colouring, and eventually advanced creative work. There’s a strong emphasis on building confidence with real clients in a supervised environment. Assessment is ongoing, with feedback to help you improve throughout the course.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I become a qualified hairdresser in the UK?

To become a hairdresser in the UK, complete a recognised Level 2 or Level 3 NVQ or Diploma through a college, academy, or apprenticeship. Most salons require these qualifications as a minimum for employment.

Are apprenticeships better than full-time courses?

It depends on your learning style. Apprenticeships offer paid, hands-on experience and direct job links, while college or academy courses provide structured learning and a broader experience. Both lead to the same qualifications.

How much do hairdressing courses cost in the UK?

It depends on the provider, the level and what is included. College courses are often free for 16 to 18-year-olds, private academies set their own fees, and apprenticeship training costs the apprentice nothing. Check each provider’s fee page before you apply.

Which is the best hairdressing school in the UK?

There is no single 'best' school—it depends on your location, budget, and career goals. Research course reviews, accreditation, facilities, and industry links to find the right fit for you.

Can I study hairdressing part-time or as an adult?

Yes, many colleges and private academies across the United Kingdom offer part-time, evening, and weekend courses. Mature learners are welcome and may find flexible options especially suitable.
