Hair Care Tips for Dry Scalp

13 November 2026

The best hair care tips for dry scalp are simple: wash with a gentle shampoo and lukewarm water, don’t over-wash, go easy on heat and harsh products, and keep the scalp comfortable through cold, windy weather and dry indoor heating. This guide explains the common causes, what you can try at home, how to choose products, and when flaking or itching is worth showing to a pharmacist or GP.

Quick answer: The best hair care tips for dry scalp include switching to gentle, sulphate-free shampoos, using natural oils, limiting heat styling, exfoliating weekly, and picking gentle products made for dry or sensitive scalps. If flaking or itching carries on, a pharmacist or GP can help work out the cause.

Key Takeaways

  • Use moisturising shampoos for dry hair and avoid harsh ingredients to prevent further dryness.

  • A consistent, gentle scalp care routine helps reduce flaking and itching.

  • Regular exfoliation removes dead skin and product build-up, supporting scalp health.

  • Professional advice from a hairdresser or pharmacist helps you choose the right products.

  • Persistent flaking, redness or soreness is worth checking with a pharmacist or GP.

What Causes Dry Scalp in the UK?

Common causes of dry scalp

Understanding the common causes of dry scalp is the first step to tackling the problem. In the United Kingdom, fluctuating temperatures, cold winds, and dry indoor heating contribute to scalp dehydration. Other factors include frequent washing, overuse of styling products, ingredients like sulphates and alcohol, and underlying skin conditions such as eczema or psoriasis. Stress may make flaking worse too, leading many to ask: why is my scalp so dry?

Recognising dry scalp symptoms and solutions

The main dry scalp symptoms are itching, a tight feeling and small white flakes. Dandruff causes white flakes too, and the NHS notes that with dandruff your scalp may also feel dry and itchy, so the two are easy to confuse. The right fix depends on the cause, but it usually starts with a gentler routine.

Practical Hair Care Tips for Dry Scalp Relief

How to moisturise a dry scalp effectively

The most effective hair care tips for dry scalp focus on restoring moisture. Switch to moisturising shampoos for dry hair that are sulphate-free and enriched with ingredients like glycerin, shea butter, or oat extract. After washing, gently towel-dry hair and apply a lightweight, leave-in conditioner to the scalp. Regularly use a light oil such as jojoba, warmed in your hands and massaged gently into the scalp before washing. Daily routines for scalp comfort

Gentle daily care is key for lasting results. Wash your hair no more than 2–3 times per week with lukewarm water to avoid stripping natural oils. Avoid scratching or using nails when shampooing—instead, use your fingertips to massage the scalp gently. Minimise the use of heated styling tools and always use a heat protectant. Allow hair to air-dry when possible and avoid tight hairstyles that can cause further irritation. At-Home Care for a Dry Scalp

Gentle at-home options

For mild dryness, many people prefer simple at-home options, such as a little plain aloe vera gel or a light oil before washing. There is little firm evidence behind most home remedies, so keep things simple. Always patch-test anything new on a small area first and stop if it stings, reddens or itches.

Scalp exfoliation techniques for dry skin

Some people like gentle scalp exfoliation techniques to lift loose flakes and product build-up. A soft silicone scalp brush used while shampooing is the gentlest option; go lightly, no more than once a week, and skip it if your scalp is sore or broken. Itchy scalp relief methods

To ease an itchy scalp, try rinsing with cool rather than hot water, avoid fragranced styling products, and don’t scratch. If the itch persists, or your scalp becomes red, swollen or sore, speak to a pharmacist or GP.

Choosing the Best Scalp Care Products in the UK

What to look for in scalp care products

When choosing scalp care products in the UK, look for ones labelled for dry or sensitive scalps, and check the ingredients for hydrating elements like panthenol and glycerin. Fragrance-free options are worth trying if your scalp is easily irritated. If you have allergies or very sensitive skin, a pharmacist can suggest suitable products.

Ask before you buy

There is plenty of choice on the high street and online. If you’re in doubt, ask your hairdresser or pharmacist which products suit your hair type, how often to use them, and what to avoid.

When flaking might be dandruff

If flaking doesn’t settle, it may be dandruff rather than a dry scalp. The NHS suggests an anti-dandruff shampoo containing one or more of ketoconazole, coal tar or salicylic acid for up to 4 weeks, and a pharmacist can advise on how to use it. The NHS says to see a GP if things have not improved after 4 weeks, if your scalp is very itchy, red or swollen, or if you have flaky, itchy patches elsewhere on your face or body.

Professional and Salon Treatments for Dry Scalp

Salon scalp treatments

Some salons offer scalp treatments, such as cleansing and moisturising masks with a scalp massage. They can feel lovely, but they are a comfort treatment, not a medical one. Prices vary widely, so ask for the price and what is included before you book.

What to expect at a professional appointment

A salon scalp treatment usually starts with a consultation, followed by cleansing, a mask or conditioning treatment, and massage. You should also get aftercare advice on products and routine. Tell your stylist about any sensitivity, broken skin or skin condition before they start.

When to see a pharmacist or GP

If home care doesn’t help, or you notice severe redness, swelling or sores, see a pharmacist or GP. They can check for skin conditions such as dandruff or psoriasis and suggest the right treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my scalp so dry?

Common causes include cold weather, central heating, washing too often and harsh hair products. Skin conditions can play a part too. Switch to gentle, moisturising products, and speak to a pharmacist or GP if it carries on.

What are the best treatments for dry scalp?

A gentle sulphate-free shampoo, lukewarm water, less heat styling and a light oil before washing help many people. If it is actually dandruff, the NHS suggests an anti-dandruff shampoo for up to 4 weeks.

How do I know if I have dry scalp or dandruff?

They can look alike: both can cause white flakes and itching, and the NHS says a scalp with dandruff may also feel dry. If anti-dandruff shampoo hasn’t helped after 4 weeks, or your scalp is red or swollen, see a GP. A pharmacist can advise in the meantime.

Do salons offer scalp treatments?

Yes, some salons and hair specialists offer scalp treatments, such as cleansing masks and massage. Ask what is included and the price before booking, and remember they are a comfort treatment rather than a medical one.

Which UK hair products are best for dry scalp?

Look for gentle, sulphate-free products labelled for dry or sensitive scalps, and try fragrance-free options if your scalp is easily irritated. A pharmacist can suggest products if you have allergies.
