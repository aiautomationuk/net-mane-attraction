Hand Washing With Cold Pressed Soap

9 November 2026

Hand washing with cold pressed soap is simple: wet your hands, work the bar into a good lather, wash every part of your hands for about 20 seconds, rinse and dry well. The real difference with a handmade bar is how you look after it, because a cold pressed soap lasts longest when it can dry out between uses.

Quick answer: Lather the bar between wet hands, wash palms, backs, fingers, thumbs and nails for around 20 seconds, then rinse and dry. Keep the soap on a draining dish away from running water so it stays firm. If you have sensitive skin, read the ingredients and try a new bar on a small area first.

Key Takeaways

  • Cold pressed soap is made slowly and left to cure before it is sold.

  • Good hand washing is about technique and time, not how much soap you use.

  • A draining soap dish makes a handmade bar last much longer.

  • Boxed bars make a thoughtful, simple gift.

What Is Cold Pressed Soap?

Cold pressed soap, often called cold process soap, is made by mixing oils and butters with lye at low temperatures. The mixture is poured into moulds, cut into bars and then left to cure for several weeks. During that time the lye reacts with the oils to become soap, and the bar hardens.

Makers choose their own oils, butters and scents, so every bar feels a little different. Many people enjoy them for their lather and fragrance, and for the small pleasure of using something made by hand.

How to Wash Your Hands Properly

The soap matters less than the way you use it. Follow these steps each time:

  1. Wet your hands with clean running water.

  2. Lather the bar between your palms for a few seconds, then put it back on its dish.

  3. Wash every part: palms, backs of the hands, between the fingers, thumbs, fingertips and nails.

  4. Keep going for about 20 seconds, roughly the time it takes to hum Happy Birthday twice.

  5. Rinse well so no soap is left on your skin.

  6. Dry thoroughly with a clean towel.

Give the bar a quick rinse before you put it down, so the next person picks up a clean bar.

Making a Handmade Bar Last

A cold pressed bar is softer when it sits in water. Left in a puddle by the tap, it turns mushy and wears away quickly. A few small habits help:

  • Use a draining dish with slats or holes so water can run off.

  • Keep it away from the shower spray or the edge of the sink where it gets splashed.

  • Cut a large bar in half and keep one piece in its box until you need it.

  • Store spare bars somewhere cool and dry, out of direct sunlight.

Choosing a Bar

Read the label. A good maker lists the ingredients, including any fragrance or essential oils. If you prefer something gentle, look for a bar with a short ingredients list and no added scent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my soap bar going soft?

It is sitting in water. Move it to a dish that drains and keep it away from splashes.

How long does a cold pressed soap bar last?

It depends on the size of the bar, how often it is used and how dry it is kept between uses. A dry bar lasts much longer than one left in a puddle. If you would like some help with this, get in touch with our team.

Is liquid soap or bar soap better for hand washing?

Both work when you wash for long enough and cover every part of your hands. Choose the one you'll actually enjoy using.
